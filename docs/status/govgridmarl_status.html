Power-flow analysis · 18:00

Newton–Raphson · AC · S_base 100 MVA
How Newton–Raphson is used: each hour the agents' curtailment decisions set the P and Q at the 11 load buses; the environment then solves the nodal equations P_i(V,θ) = P_i^spec, Q_i(V,θ) = Q_i^spec for the 13 unknown angles and 11 unknown |V| by iterating the Jacobian until the largest mismatch is below 1e-8 MVA (2–3 iterations from a warm start). The result gives the load balance shown above — generation = load + I²R losses, with the slack bus B1 absorbing the difference — plus the branch loadings and bus voltages that feed the constraint costs.

2Bus selection & dimensions the electrical argument for where the households sit, with the numbers

Why the clusters sit on these buses

+5 MW sensitivity test at every load bus
Electrical argument: 5 MW is added at one load bus at a time and the power flow re-solved. The six LV buses behind the transformers (B9–B14) show 5–10× the local voltage sensitivity and 5–15× the branch-loading sensitivity of the 135 kV buses — every MW drawn there flows through T4-7 / T4-9 / T5-6 and the radial 9-10-11 and 9-14-13 loops. Generator buses hold their voltage by construction. Residential flexibility placed at B9–B14 first therefore has the largest effect on the constraints Phase 2 enforces; the remaining clusters fill B4, B3, B1, B2, B5 in order. Two views of the same day. Grid as a whole is the Phase-1 demand-response objective: residential peak, fairness of the burden, constraint violations. Energy-share view asks what the renewables did: how much of consumption they covered, how much surplus had to be exported, the peak net import from the slack, and the worst branch loading. The scenario buttons switch the generation mix; the policy toggle in section 1 switches the controller.

Sweep · whole-grid KPIs vs energy-share KPIs across scenarios

peak reduction % (grid as a whole)renewables consumed % of consumptionmax branch loading %100 % = rating◆ surplus exported, MWh (right axis)
What changes with the mix: up to ≈ 40 % renewable share every MWh is consumed locally and losses fall (generation moves closer to load). Beyond that the shape matters more than the share: a PV-heavy 80 % mix exports a large midday surplus, reverses flows on the LV loops and overloads a branch, while a wind-heavy 80 % mix (70–90 % wind) is absorbed almost entirely because the wind farm at B2 sits on the 135 kV backbone next to the slack. The whole-grid bars are flat: the Phase-1 policies see the PV curve but not the wind injection or the net load, and their reward targets the residential evening peak, so peak reduction and Jain do not move with the mix and the midday surplus is left untouched. That is the measured baseline the energy-share-aware controller of Phase 2 must beat — with high PV the useful action is to shift load into midday, and fairness becomes "who gets the cheap midday energy".
