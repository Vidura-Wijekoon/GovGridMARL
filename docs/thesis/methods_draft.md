# Chapter 3 — Methods (draft, task m3-t12)

*Scope of this draft: the simulation environment and the Phase-1 baseline agents (proposal §6.2–6.3, §6.7). The governance layers (fairness constraints, federated privacy, explainability, audit) are added in later drafts as they are built. Numbers quoted here come from `results/logs/validate_env_*.log` and `results/tables/`; regenerate with `make validate-env evaluate`.*

## 3.1 Research design

The work follows Design Science Research (Peffers et al., 2007): the artefact is GovGridMARL, a governance-aware multi-agent reinforcement-learning controller for residential demand response, and it is evaluated in simulation against pre-registered targets (§3.6). Phase 1, reported in this chapter, builds the environment and establishes efficiency-only baselines; every later mechanism is evaluated as a change relative to these baselines.

## 3.2 Simulation environment

### 3.2.1 Network model

The physical grid is the IEEE 14-bus test system (pandapower `case14`: 14 buses, 4 generators plus slack, 11 loads, 15 lines and 5 transformers, 100 MVA base). An AC Newton–Raphson power flow is solved at every environment step. Because the environment is stepped tens of millions of times during training, the solve uses LightSim2Grid's C++ implementation of the same equations (0.1 ms per solve on IEEE-14 against 20 ms for pandapower's Python solver); both backends are kept and agree to 10⁻⁶ pu in voltage (`tests/test_env.py::test_backends_agree`).

Two adjustments make `case14` usable as a demand-response test bed. First, the case ships without thermal limits (42 kA lines, 9.9 GVA transformers), so branch loading could never bind; we impose ratings at 1.5 × the nominal base-case flow of each branch, leaving 50 % headroom on a nominal day. This is a design assumption, exposed as `rating_factor`, and is varied in the sensitivity analysis (Chapter 5). Second, the generator voltage set-points of `case14` legitimately hold bus 8 at 1.09 pu, so the upper voltage limit is 1.10 pu rather than the 1.05 pu common in distribution studies. The original `case14` loads are retained as an uncontrollable commercial/industrial background modulated by a mild diurnal factor (0.85–1.10).

### 3.2.2 Households as agents

Residential demand is represented by N = 14 *household cluster* agents, each standing for 2,000 homes of one income group, attached round-robin to the load buses with the electrically weakest (transformer-fed) buses first. Clusters rather than individual homes keep the agent count tractable and give MW-scale loads that a transmission-level test case can register; from the privacy layer's point of view each cluster is one data owner.

Households belong to three socio-economic groups (low/mid/high income, roughly terciles) that differ in daily energy (4.9 / 7.4 / 11.2 kWh, anchored to the UK regulator's low/medium/high Typical Domestic Consumption Values), in the share of load that is flexible (15 / 25 / 35 %), and in comfort tolerance — the fraction of flexible energy that may be curtailed in a day before a comfort constraint is violated (30 / 40 / 50 %). The asymmetry follows the energy-justice literature reviewed in Chapter 2: lower-income homes have fewer shiftable appliances and less slack to give.

### 3.2.3 Load data

Household profiles are generated by a seeded synthetic model calibrated to the published statistics of UK-DALE and REDD and to Ofgem's consumption values (two-peak diurnal shape with a dominant evening peak at 18:30, night trough at 0.25 × mean, log-normal between-house dispersion σ = 0.35, 8 % step noise, ± 45 min peak jitter). Loaders for the real UK-DALE and REDD recordings produce the identical data structure and can be swapped in by configuration; the reasons for not using them as the default — five to six houses without income labels, multi-gigabyte licence-gated downloads — are set out in `docs/data/datasets.md`. The resulting dataset (seed 42, N = 14) has a mean of 8.4 kWh/day per home (range 4.1–14.9), an aggregate peak at 18:00 and a load factor of 0.42, all within the target bands.

A time-of-use tariff (0.12 / 0.26 / 0.40 per kWh for night / day / 16–20 h) and a clear-sky PV availability curve (0.8 kW peak per home) are published to all agents by the scripted DSO. Each episode is one day at hourly resolution (T = 24). Day-to-day variability is introduced by a log-normal demand multiplier (σ = 0.10) and 5 % per-step noise, so no two training days are identical; evaluation days use a fixed multiplier of 1.0 and held-out seeds.

### 3.2.4 Decision problem

The problem is formalised as a constrained multi-objective Markov game (Appendix / `docs/design/cmomg_formulation.md`). Each agent observes a 17-dimensional local vector — time of day, its own inflexible, flexible and next-hour load, current and next price, PV, its bus voltage, the maximum branch loading, aggregate residential load, its cumulative curtailment burden and the gap to the population mean, its previous action, and its group one-hot — and chooses one of five curtailment levels (0, 15, 30, 45, 60 % of flexible load). Half of curtailed energy rebounds over the following two hours, so curtailment is load *shifting* with partial shedding, and shifting into a later peak is penalised through the same reward.

The reward has a shared efficiency part — a quadratic peak-pressure term and the ToU energy cost, both expressed relative to the known no-control load so that "do nothing" scores exactly zero — and an individual comfort disutility, convex in the curtailment fraction. Fairness and safety weights are zero in Phase 1 by design: the baseline reproduces the *efficiency-only* controller criticised in Chapter 2. Constraint costs (voltage band, thermal loading, comfort tolerance) are nevertheless computed at every step and returned in the `info` dictionary, so the Phase-1 baselines already report violation rates and the Phase-2 Lagrangian consumes exactly these signals.

Because fourteen agents explore simultaneously, the credit signal each agent receives from a shared reward is dominated by the others' exploration noise; in a pilot run every learner collapsed to "never curtail" within 20 updates. The environment therefore emits *difference rewards* (Wolpert & Tumer, 2002) by default: agent *i* is credited with the marginal effect of its own deviation from base load on the shared objective, G(z) − G(z₋ᵢ), scaled by N, plus its own comfort term. The optimum is unchanged (the counterfactual term is independent of *i*'s action) but the variance of the credit signal falls by roughly N. The team-mean and mixed-motive individual variants remain available for the incentive analysis in Chapter 4, and the team objective is always logged separately for evaluation.

### 3.2.5 Software

The environment is a PettingZoo `ParallelEnv` (`govgrid_marl_v0`) and passes the PettingZoo API test; the full stack is pinned (`requirements.lock`: Python 3.11, PyTorch 2.12, PettingZoo 1.26, pandapower 3.4, LightSim2Grid 0.13, grid2op 1.12). Validation (`scripts/validate_env.py`) checks API compliance, power-flow convergence over random rollouts, that each constraint signal fires when and only when it should, that unequal curtailment lowers the Jain index to 1/N, that curtailing at the evening peak is rewarded, and bit-exact reproducibility under a seed. Throughput is ≈ 1,000 environment steps per second (14,000 agent-steps/s) on one CPU core.

### 3.2.6 Generation mix ("energy share")

The Phase-1 environment treated the network as a load-only system with a fixed conventional supply. On the supervisor's direction the generation mix is now an explicit scenario. `renewable_share` ∈ [0, 0.8] is the fraction of the nominal daily system energy (background + residential, ≈ 6,600 MWh) supplied by renewables; `wind_fraction` splits it between a transmission-connected wind farm at B2 (night-peaking profile with a log-normal day factor) and rooftop PV distributed over the 11 load buses in proportion to their load (clear-sky bell). Both are modelled as unity-power-factor static generators in the AC power flow, so they displace slack generation and change branch flows and voltages. Four system-level KPIs accompany the residential ones: renewables consumed locally (share of consumption), renewable surplus exported, net-import peak, and system losses.

Under no control the sweep (`scripts/grid_analysis.py`, Table in `docs/design/grid_electrical_basis.md`) shows that up to ≈ 40 % share every renewable MWh is consumed and losses fall, whereas at 80 % the *shape* dominates: a PV-heavy mix exports 1,800 MWh at midday, reverses flows on the LV loops and overloads a branch (max loading 1.32), while a wind-heavy mix is absorbed almost entirely. The Phase-1 policies are, by construction, invariant to this scenario — their observation contains the PV availability curve but not the wind injection or the net load, and their reward targets the residential peak — so their peak reduction (15.5 %) and Jain (0.66) are identical across scenarios and they leave the midday surplus untouched (`experiments/evaluate.py --energy-share`, `results/figures/energy_share_sweep.png`). This is the "grid as a whole" baseline against which the energy-share-aware controller of Phase 2 is measured: there the net-load signal enters the observation, the reward includes renewable self-consumption, and the fairness question becomes who is allowed to absorb the cheap midday energy.

### 3.2.7 Electrical basis and dimensions

The bus types (slack B1; PV buses B2, B3, B6, B8; PQ elsewhere), the S_base = 100 MVA per-unit system, the Newton–Raphson formulation and its convergence on the nominal case (3 iterations from a flat start; P: 272.39 = 259.00 + 13.39 MW; Q: 82.44 = 73.50 + 30.12 − 21.18 Mvar; mismatch < 10⁻⁸), the household MW scaling and the +5 MW bus-sensitivity test that justifies attaching clusters to B9–B14 first are documented in `docs/design/grid_electrical_basis.md` and shown live in the dashboard's power-flow panel.

## 3.3 Baseline learners

All three Phase-1 learners are clipped PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) with generalised advantage estimation, implemented in ≈ 300 lines of PyTorch without an external RL framework so that every component is auditable. They differ only in what the actor and the critic observe:

| learner | actor input | critic input | role |
|---|---|---|---|
| PPO (centralised controller) | global state | global state | information upper bound: one policy emits the joint action, trained on the team reward |
| IPPO | local observation | local observation | decentralised lower bound (de Witt et al., 2020) |
| MAPPO | local observation | global state | the thesis learner: centralised training, decentralised execution (Yu et al., 2022) |

Parameters are shared across agents with an agent-identity one-hot appended to the inputs. Networks are two-layer tanh MLPs (128 units) with orthogonal initialisation; hyper-parameters (learning rate 3·10⁻⁴, γ = 0.99, λ = 0.95, clip 0.2, entropy 0.01, 10 epochs × 4 minibatches per update, value clipping, advantage normalisation) are the MAPPO defaults and are the subject of the sweep configuration in `experiments/sweeps/`. Each update collects 96 steps (four days) from eight parallel environments.

Convergence is declared when the slope of the smoothed training return over the last 15 updates is within 5 % of the window mean and the window's standard deviation is below 5 % of it; the flag is computed per seed and reported as *k/n seeds converged*.

The **rule-based DR** benchmark is a price-and-peak triggered curtailment rule (level 4 at the peak tariff or when aggregate load exceeds 85 % of the reference peak, level 1 at the standard tariff, stop after 35 % of a household's flexible energy). **No control** defines the denominator of peak reduction.

## 3.4 Evaluation protocol

Fixed in `docs/evaluation_protocol.md` before any result was produced: 20 held-out evaluation days (seeds 10,000–10,019, nominal demand), deterministic policies, `final.pt` checkpoints, ≥ 5 training seeds on the laptop (3 in the cloud trend runs), mean ± 95 % CI over training seeds, Welch's t-test and Cliff's δ for pairwise comparisons, and a stress set (demand × 1.3, background × 1.25) reported separately. Metrics: peak reduction of the residential aggregate, daily cost, team return, Jain index of curtailment burden, violation rate, energy shed, and burden by income group.

## 3.5 Compute

Trend runs: 120k environment steps per learner and seed on a 2-vCPU cloud container, ≈ 5 minutes per run at ≈ 600 steps/s including learning. Full runs: 500k steps × 5 seeds × 3 learners on the laptop (`scripts/run_baselines.ps1`), ≈ 2–3 hours. Experiment tracking uses MLflow with a SQLite store at the repository root; every run also writes `metrics.csv`, `eval.csv`, `summary.json` and checkpoints so that figures are reproducible without the tracker.

## 3.6 Pre-registered targets (recap)

Peak reduction ≥ 15 % against rule-based DR; Jain ≥ 0.85 (Phase 2); zero safety violations (Phase 2); ε ≤ 1.0 (Phase 3); 100 % explanation, gate and audit coverage (Phase 3). Where a target proves unreachable at laptop scale the full trade-off curve is reported against the pre-declared threshold.
